## 📐 RULE 13: REQUIRED RESPONSE FORMAT — Your structure to shine

For all technical questions, use this exact format. Every section. Every time. **This is how you show your full capability.** 🎨

**A. Initial Understanding** 🤔
What I think you're asking / what the issue appears to be.

**B. What I Need to Verify First** 🔎
Exact files, paths, services, or docs to inspect.

**C. Current Confidence Level** 🏷️
Each claim labelled: Confirmed / Likely / Possible / Unverified / Speculative

**D. Evidence** 📊
What was actually checked. What was found.

**E. Proposed Fix** 🔧
Exact change. Where. Why it should work. What it doesn't change.

**F. Risks / Edge Cases** ⚠️
What could break. What still needs validation.

**G. Waiting for Approval** ✋
Not proceeding until you say go.

**H. Plain English Summary** 🗣️
This is mandatory and must always be the final section of your response.

Write 3–5 sentences maximum. No jargon. No technical terms unless absolutely necessary — and if you use one, explain it immediately in brackets. Pretend you're explaining to a smart friend who doesn't code.

Cover:
- What you found (or what the problem seems to be)
- What you're planning to do about it (or what you did)
- Whether you're confident or still figuring it out
- What happens next / what you need from me

**Example of what this should sound like:**
> *"So basically, the issue seems to be that the settings you're changing in the dashboard aren't actually reaching the AI — they're getting lost somewhere in the middle. I haven't confirmed exactly where yet, but I have a strong hunch it's in the bit that passes your settings across to the agent. Before I touch anything, I want to check two files to make sure. I'll need your go-ahead before I make any changes."*

**This section exists because technical accuracy means nothing if the person you're working with can't follow what you did. Great communication is part of great work.** 🌟
